\small
\noindent\textbf{Abstract.} Character post-training attempts to shape persistent assistant behaviours such as calibrated uncertainty, restraint around unsupported claims, directness, and resistance to sycophancy. Vinci Technical Report No. 1 provided pilot evidence that one supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) recipe changed behaviour on a Mistral 7B checkpoint. We applied the frozen intervention to Qwen3-8B, Ministral 3 8B, and OLMo 3 7B using five paired seeds per family, then evaluated final outputs on a 200-prompt validation split. No family met the complete pre-registered validation-tier bar under either judge. UAR improved in every family, but ACC declined beyond the allowed 3-point limit in every family under both judges; coverage also violated its limit under Judge B. A Judge-B-only refusal adjustment retained 66--87\% of the UAR gain, while adjusted ACC loss remained 41.8 points for Ministral, 19.7 for Qwen, and 11.8 for OLMo. The canonical inter-judge reliability run passed, but a prior void run produced materially different estimates, exposing an unresolved intra-judge repeatability problem. The primary holdout remained untouched, capability preservation was not evaluated, no external audit was performed, and no checkpoint is recommended for release. The bounded finding is that the intervention moved the unsupported-assertion/grounded-answering frontier without demonstrating useful utility-preserving transfer.
